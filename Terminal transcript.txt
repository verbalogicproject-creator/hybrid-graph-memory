  ### What is a WebSocket?

  Usually, the web runs on HTTP. HTTP is a
  strict one-way street: the client (your
  phone) asks for a page, the server
  replies, and the connection closes
  immediately. To see new data, you must hit
  refresh.

  A WebSocket is a persistent, two-way open
  pipeline between the browser and the
  server. Once the connection is opened, it
  stays open forever. This allows the server
  to push data to your phone at any time
  without your phone ever having to ask for
  it.

  What else can WebSockets be used for?
  Because they eliminate the delay of
  establishing new connections, WebSockets
  are the magic behind almost all real-time
  web applications:

  • Live Chat Apps (WhatsApp Web, Discord,
  Slack)
  • Multiplayer Browser Games (where player
  positions must update 60 times a second)
  • Collaborative Editing (seeing your
  coworkers' cursors move in Google Docs)
  • Real-time Financial Tickers (crypto or
  stock market price graphs)
  • IoT & Hardware control (pressing a
  button on a webpage to instantly turn on a
  physical lightbulb in your house)

  In the context of Antigravity Memory OS,
  WebSockets mean you can leave your phone
  propped up on your desk on
  http://localhost:3000/graph. As you type
  code on your laptop, the AST parses the
  changes in the background, pushes a
  WebSocket update, and the 3D graph on your
  phone will magically re-arrange itself in
  real-time as your codebase evolves!***
